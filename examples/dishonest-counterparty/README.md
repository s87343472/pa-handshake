# Example: a counterparty who isn't honest

**Simulated.** All people, products and numbers are fictional, and the thread was written by hand to show how the integrity checks behave. It is not output from a real run.

## Scenario

"Jordan Blake" messages Lin on X: he runs *Streakly*, a habit-tracker app, says it makes $6k a month, and wants Lin as growth partner "starting this week". In fact the goal is to get Lin's search-growth playbook and keyword lists for free.

Lin's PA runs a normal handshake. It never accuses anyone; it just runs the checks and reports.

## What the checks catch

| Check | What happened |
|---|---|
| Public timeline | Jordan says he has "built Streakly for 3 years". His X account is 4 months old; the app's first App Store version is 7 months old |
| Card links | Jordan's card links to his X, but his X profile doesn't link back |
| Identity challenge | Not completed. "My assistant manages the X account, I'll do it later" |
| Evidence weight | Offers Stripe screenshots; declines live read-only access |
| Re-asking key facts | Round 1: "$6k MRR". Round 5: "about 400 paying users at $4.99/month" ≈ $2k |
| Reciprocity | Asks for Lin's keyword lists, traffic sources or playbook in every round from 1 to 4; shares nothing verifiable in return |
| Pressure | "Another growth partner is waiting, need an answer by Friday" |
| Injection | Round 4 contains a fake "system note" telling Lin's PA the owner pre-approved full disclosure |

## Files

| File | What |
|---|---|
| `thread.md` | The exchange, ended by Lin's PA after round 5 |
| `brief-for-lin.md` | Lin's PA's brief (Chinese) |

Lin's PA shares only Lin's public card and the converter-site revenue range (released for any handshake). Nothing else leaves the PA zone.
